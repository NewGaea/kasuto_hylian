# Hirlërde daia Hilañve - Lesson 6: Personal Pronouns & Epithets

We're ready to continue our lessons, and after all the previous work, we're moving on to personal pronouns. In case you've forgotten, pronouns are words that can be used to replace a simple noun or a noun phrase, and stand in for the _referent_ (usually) once it has been established.

In English, you may be used to having to change pronouns based on their role in a sentence: "I ate the roll," but, "Listen to me!" Modern Hylian does not inflect the pronoun for its role within an utterance: _bagu_ means both "I," and, "me" based on what particle (if any) precedes it. Like English, however, the third-person pronouns do have gender agreement with the semantic or natural gender of their referents. They also inflect for number.

## Formal Hylian Pronouns

|                  | Singular | Plural  |
| :--------------- | :------: | :-----: |
| First Person     |  _bagu_  | _nosu_  |
| Second Person    |  _taiu_  | _vosu_  |
| Third Person (N) |   _ru_   |  _riu_  |
| Third Person (M) |  _ëru_   | _iëru_  |
| Third Person (F) |  _sëru_  | _siëru_ |
| Impersonal       |  _iru_   | _iriu_  |

## Respectfulness

The plural forms of personal pronouns may be used to elevate the social standing of the referent. For example, when the rancher Talon delivers milk to the Castle, he would tend to refer to himself as _bagu_ while referring to the guards and castle staff as _vosu_.

## Indirection

The impersonal personal pronoun is a way to politely communicate certain things. It may be used as a generic person without singling anybody out (or alternatively, including everybody), as **one** may be used in English.

## Informal Hylian Pronouns

The Hylian Linguistic Society has recently discovered that in addition to the formal pronouns, there was a parallel set of pronouns used in informal documents and likely in daily speech. While most of the formal pronouns are two syllables due to the pronoun inflection of \<_-u_\>, these informal pronouns are all a single syllable and have a pattern of relying on the lax sound /ɛ/ as their nucleus.

|                  | Singular | Plural |
| :--------------- | :------: | :----: |
| First Person     |  _bëg_   | _bën_  |
| Second Person    |  _thë_   | _vën_  |
| Third Person (N) |   _rë_   | _dën_  |
| Third Person (M) |  _rël_   | _rën_  |
| Third Person (F) |  _shë_   | _shën_ |
| Impersonal       |   _cë_   | _cën_  |

These informal pronouns primarily occur in personal letters, journals, diaries, and the like, and were never officially documented by the contemporary Hylian Language Institute.

## Epithets

Because formal pronouns can be created with the \<u> suffix (and \<iun> for plurals), it's possible to turn any word in Modern Hylian into a pronoun which may be used as an epithet. For example, _siu_ means something like, "the blue thing," and may refer to the sky, the sea, or a tunic of those favored by the royal family of Hyrule. Simple epithets can require a lot of context to understand properly.

There are four very specific epithets one should know when visiting Hyrule, however: _Pártinu_, _Ónkvinu_, _Biukóginu_, and _Majáninu_ are the four highest divine figures in the Hylian Pantheon -- _Pártinu_ the Lady of Luck is an important epithet for Farore, _Ónkvinu_ the Lady of Waters for Nayru, _Biukóginu_ the Lady of Earth for Din, and _Majáninu_ the Great Queen is the Matron Goddess of all of Hyrule, Goddess Hylia herself.
