# Hirlërde daia Hilañve - Chapter 3: Basic Hylian Nouns

Now that we’ve covered the basic structure of statements and verbs, it’s time to learn how to use nouns in Modern Hylian. Nouns, or _sonën_ in Modern Hylian, are words that represent things: concrete things like people, places, and objects, and abstract things like ideas, forces, and emotions.

Hylian nouns are easy to inflect as you start learning, because they only have two forms: Singular and plural. We will not address _proper_ nouns (like personal names) in this lesson.

## Singular Nouns

The least inflected noun form is the singular, and it always ends with the _-e_ suffix:

---
- **lërde:** _book,_ _letter, note_
- **diare:** _gift,_ _present_
- **koeme:** _food_
---

There’s no trick to it, either. Of note, you might remember that _diarag_ is the verb “to give, giving” – by cutting off the verbal inflection and replacing it with _-e_ you get a noun related to the same idea. The same rule applies in reverse to make a verb!

## Plural Nouns

Plural nouns refer to groups of things. If there are two or more countable objects in a group, you use the plural. To form a plural noun, replace the _-e_ suffix with _-ën_.

---
- **lërdën:** _books, letters, notes_
- **grohën**: _grains, seeds, kernels_
- **vatën:** _eggs_
---

In addition to this, Modern Hylian uses plural forms for uncountable concrete nouns. For example:

---
```ngloss
\ex Lïnkera koemes ab rausën.
\ft Link ate (some) rice.
```
---

## New Words

Here are a few new words to play with between this lesson and the next:

| Hylian    | English             |
| --------- | ------------------- |
| _piante_  | parent              |
| _hiose_   | child               |
| _akime_   | friend              |
| _amkane_  | love                |
| _tiudre_  | midday, afternoon   |
| _malsëte_ | meal, plate of food |

Modern Hylian has no grammatical gender, and this principle extends to its core vocabulary. It doesn’t have specific words for things that have a distinction by sex or gender, so at its core, it has “parent” and “child” without specific terms for “father, mother, son, daughter.”
