# Nioshe kaz Hilañve | Introduction to the Hylian Language

The purpose of the Hylian Linguistic Society (HLS) is to research the modern and historical languages of the people of Hyrule and its neighbors, near and far.

In this project, we are reconstructing a form of the ancient tongue of Hyrule which was spoken around, and especially following, the **Unification War** in which King Rogël Antons Hyrule brought the lands of Central Hyrule, Lanayru, Eldin, Faron, and Gerudo into a unified kingdom in which all residents had full citizenship, and no race of Hylian was seen as superior to any other. Under King Rogël, the Kingdom of Hyrule reached from modern-day Tanagar Canyon in the northwest to Death Mountain in the northeast, and from the Gerudo Desert in the Southeast to the Lanayru Sea in the east.

Following the Unification War, Queen Abishemia pushed for cultural and linguistic education to be made available across the land, and with King Rogël they called on the wisest scholars to reform the plethora of local writing systems into a single unified form, which when finished, became the *aktaite* (alphabet, written script) of the era: a rigid rectilinear script with minimal curves or diagonals.

From our analysis, it appears that Neo-Modern Hylian was a unified form of Modern Hylian, attempting to both erase dialectal differences as a sign of class or rank, while embracing them for the distinctions they could provide. According to the documents we have uncovered from the time, the Hylian Language Institute was made up of scholars and lay-folk who met regularly to document -- and to update documents describing -- the Neo-Modern Hylian language. While few of the original documents survive, these documents were faithfully preserved and copied by scholars for centuries, if not millenia, after the peak of Neo-Modern Hylian as it shifted into newer tongues.

## An Overview of *Hilañve*

Neo-Modern Hylian is a highly regularized language in which almost every word is strictly marked for its role in an utterance, and where even the parts of an utterance are marked up front to define their role -- words inflect for role and number, and every object in a sentence begins with a particle to indicate what is about to be said.

One of the simplest sentences we can use to demonstrate this is:

> *Lïnkera goeares ab raz malsete dëna.*
> 
> Link ate a small meal.

You can see here that the subject, *Lïnkera* "Link," and the verb *goeares* "ate, had eaten" don't have any special marking, but after the verb, we have ***ab*** which tells us the next phrase is the direct object, followed by *raz malsete dëna*: "a small meal." There are several other particles which mark different types of other phrases, and particles always come at the start of the phrase they apply to.

Neo-Modern Hylian is a language that addresses the Subject first, the primary Verb next, and any Objects (and secondary verbs) after the primary verb. Linguistically, this is referred to as SVO.

The order within phrases is that the most important element of a phrase appears first, followed by any qualities that the **head element** possesses. Articles are usually the first thing in a phrase after the particle, with locations generally coming before the **head** and all other modifiers after the **head**.

Compound words in Neo-Modern Hylian *tend* to take the opposite structure from what might be expected in English: the English *sunflower* would be rendered in this language as *abiushëmse* "flower-sun" -- the most important or salient part of the compound generally comes first.

Hylians use a base-10 counting system, with numbers from 0 (*sore*) to 999 (*nivëk nidëmëk nisale*), with a particle *do* which goes between numbers of higher orders such as 1.101 (*hirmale do hirëk hirsale*). Note that within every range from 0-999, the least significant digit is written first, and larger numbers must be separated with a dot of some sort to make the values clear.
