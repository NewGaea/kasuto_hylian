# emekthian's Gághïri Language

> [! INFO]
> 
> This file contains a text dump of https://www.angelfire.com/ultra/fnuh/conlangs/kokiri.html which has been proofread and edited by [Austin Springer](contributors/austin-springer.md). The information provided here is limited, but is all the publicly available documentation for this conlang.

## ## Introduction

Gágïrhi is the language of the Kokiri from The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (the Kokiri's name for themselves is also *Gágïrhi* -- it has been Anglicized (and Hylianized 😄 ) into "kokiri"). The Kokiri are a race of childlike people who live in the Kokiri forest in the east of Hyrule (if you don't know what I'm talking about, you haven't played Ocarina of Time. If you haven't played Ocarina of Time, for the love of Din, Nayru and Farore _play it_! It's one of the best video games ever made!).  

Some notable features of Gágïrhi are:

- **Gágïrhi** makes an aspirated/unaspirated distinction, and has implosives
- The language doesn't mark tense on the verb, but does mark aspect
- In shouted Gágïrhi, or in other situations where the volume level is unclear, the speaker says a drawn out "a" before speaking to indicate the "base level" of volume. This is known as a **jjíbag**.
- I realize that 4 list items isn't much, but I'm still working on Gágïrhi!

## Phonology

### Consonants

|                  |        Labial         |    Alveolar     |        Palatal        |      Uvular       |
| ---------------- | :-------------------: | :-------------: | :-------------------: | :---------------: |
| **Stops**        | \<b p bb><br>/p pʰ ɓ/ | \<d t><br>/d t/ | \<j c jj><br>/c cʰ ʄ/ | \<g gg><br>/q ʛ/  |
| **Fricatives**   |    \<f v><br>/f v/    | \<s z><br>/s z/ |   \<sh zh><br>/ʃ ʒ/   | \<kh gh><br>/χ ʁ/ |
| **Approximants** |                       |   \<r><br>/ɹ/   |      \<y><br>/j/      |   \<rh><br>/ʀ/    |

The consonants \<**bb**>, \<**jj**>, and \<**gg**> are actually implosives. \<**b**,> \<**j**> and \<**g**> are unaspirated unvoiced plosives, and \<**p**> and \<**c**> are the aspirated counterparts. There is no aspirated velar plosive, nor is there a alveolar implosive.

**r** is actually an alveolar approximant, like the "r" in American English (IPA /ɹ/). **rh** is an uvular trill, like in French.  

### Vowels

Vowels in Gágïrhi have a volume dimension. In other words, /a/ can be 2 different vowels, depending on how loud or soft it is spoken. The volume levels are numbered from 1 (low) to 5 (high).

There are three base vowels: a /a/, i /i/, and u /ɯ/. Note that the u is unrounded. The vowel by itself is at level 3 volume. An acute accent raises it one level (to 4) and an umlaut raises it two levels (to 5). So a-3, á-4, ä-5.

Similarly, a grave will lower the volume by one level, and a circumflex will lower the volume by two levels. u-3, ù-2, û-1. **Some vowel and volume combinations don't exist; there is no à, î or ú.**

> [!Alternate Notation]
> 
> For IPA notation, Austin recommends using tone markings to represent volume of the segment.
> 
> Volume can also be noted by adding the volume number of a syllable (in standard Indoarabic numerals) at the start of each syllable, though this is recommended only where neither IPA tone nor diacritics are viable.

## Verbs

All verbs in Gágïrhi have a CVC particle directly after them: **dájju _ggibb_**. The CVC particle indicates aspect, person, and mood. The first consonant indicates aspect, the vowel indicates person, and the last consonant indicates mood. Gágïrhi doesn't express tense on the verb; you have to use a word like "tomorrow."

### Aspect

Gágïrhi uses the following aspects:

- **Perfective**: indicating that the action is complete, or that it will be complete (even if the action hasn't taken place yet.
- **Imperfective**: The opposite of the perfective, indicating that the action isn't completed.
- **Inchoative**: Indicates that the action is beginning, or will begin, or has begun.
- **Cessative**: The inchoative's opposite, indicating that the action has stopped.
- **Habitual**: Indicates that the action takes place multiple times.
- **Progressive**: Indicates that the action is ongoing.

Each aspect is indicated by a different consonant in the initial place in the particle:  

| Perfective | Imperfective | Inchoative | Habitual | Progressive | Cessative |
| :--------: | :----------: | :--------: | :------: | :---------: | :-------: |
|    c--     |     t--      |    gg--    |   b--    |    jj--     |    p--    |

For example, **pakìvu**, "to dance", in the perfect aspect would be **pakìvu c--**, and in the habitual aspect would be **pakìvu b--**  

### Person

Person is marked on Gágïrhi verbs by the vowel in the CVC particle. To understand how this process works, you need to know about the Gágïrhi pronouns.

Gágïrhi uses a system of variables that can be assigned to nouns instead of pronouns. To assign a variable to a noun, you add the particle **bb-gh** right before the noun. The blank space can be either **a**, **i**, **u**, or **ü**. These four vowels serve as the variables. There is also a general pronoun word, **p-s**. The blank space can be one of the four variable vowels.

For example, take the noun **zhaggiv**. To assign it to the **ü** variable, you would say **_bbügh_ zhaggiv**. You could then refer to it as a noun by saying **p*ü*s**, or as the person of a verb by saying **pakìvu -ü-**.

Whenver the subject of a verb isn't a pronoun, but a noun, use **í** as the vowel: **zhaggiv pakìmu -í-**. Note that you could also say **bbagh zhaggiv pakìmu -a-**

| Noun | Marked a | Marked i | Marked u | Marked ü |
| :--: | :------: | :------: | :------: | :------: |
| -í-  |   -a-    |   -i-    |   -u-    |   -ü-    |

### Mood
  
Gágïrhi has several moods:

- **Indicative**: the default mood
- **Subjunctive**: used for desired states, or for statements that may or may not be true, or for hypotheticals. Often the equivalent of English "may" or "might"
- **Conditional**: used in if:then statements, often the equivalent of English "would"
- **Negative**: used to indicate that the statement is untrue.

Here are the endings for each mood:  

| Indicative | Subjunctive | Conditional | Negative |
| ---------- | ----------- | ----------- | -------- |
| ∅ (null)   | bb          | z           | gh       |

### Other Verb Markings

Gágïrhi marks several other things on the verb.

#### Definiteness

Definiteness ("the dog" is definite, "a dog" is indefinite) is marked on the verb in Gágïrhi. The default form is indefinite. To indicate definateness, you add the vowel **-ï** to the verb if it ends in a consonant: **shívâd** "a (noun) works", **shívâd*ï*** "the (noun) works". If the verb ends in a vowel, that vowel is raised to the highest volume: **jjüsá** ${\rightarrow}$ **jjüs*ä****; **pakívu** ${\rightarrow}$ **pakív*ü***.  

## Nouns

Nouns are inflected by number (singular, dual and plural)and case (nominative, accusative, dative, posessive).
#### Case

- **Nominative**: The subject of a sentance, and the unmarked form of a noun
- **Accusative**: Used for the direct object, and for some prepositions. A noun in the accusative is proceeded by the direct object particle **fï**.
- **Dative**: Used for the indirect object, and for the object of most prepositions. A noun in the dative is proceeded by the indirect object particle **âjj**.
- **Possessive**: Used to express possession, like English 's. A noun in the possessive has the suffix **-zhi**  


| Nominative | Accusative | Dative | Possessive |
| :--------: | :--------: | :----: | :--------: |
|  ∅ (null)  |   fï \*    | âjj \* |   \*-zhi   |

#### Number

The three numbers in Gágïrhi are singular, dual and plural. The singular is the default form of a noun, and is used to express that there is only one of a noun. The plural is used to express that there are more than one of a noun. Both of these are used similarly to the way they are used in English. The dual is used when talking about a pair of things (like two lips, two shoes). "Two shoes" is shoes-DUAL.

Note that the dual is not used when referring to two things that aren't a logical pair; "two bricks" that have nothing to do with each other are "two brick-PLURAL"  
#### Plural

The plural is formed by adding the suffix **-ggâ**. The dual is formed by adding the suffix **-jjí**.

|              | Nominative | Accusative |   Dative   | Possessive |
| ------------ | :--------: | :--------: | :--------: | :--------: |
| **Singular** |     \*     |   fí \*    |   âjj \*   |   \*-zhi   |
| **Dual**     |   \*-jjí   | fi \*-jjí  | âjj \*-jjí | \*-zhijjí  |
| **Plural**   |   \*-ggâ   | fi \*-ggâ  | âjj \*-ggâ | \*-zhiggâ  |

> **bbagh dâfibbû ggizhï jja**  
> _ASSIGN.A house collapses DEFINITE PROGRESSIVE-A.VARIABLE-INDICATIVE_  
> The house is collapsing.

